Framework · Problem framing

Ask the right questions before designing anything

Two open-ended product problems, one a new product and one an improvement, taken apart with 5W1H to pin down the users, the business goal, the context, the success metric and the constraints before any solution.

Frameworks (PDF, 2 slides)

The approach

For each problem I wrote the clarifying questions under Who, What, Where, When, Why and How. Each answer narrows the problem along one of five lines:

  1. Target usersWhich cohort matters most
  2. Business objectiveWhat success means for the company
  3. Product contextThe place, time and trigger of use
  4. Success metricsLeading and lagging indicators
  5. Scope and constraintsTechnical and regulatory limits

Product design: design a digital temple

A new product with no obvious user, platform or business model, so every W changes what gets built.

QuestionClarifying questions
WhoWho is the primary persona: the elderly, NRIs missing their cultural roots, or millennials who want a quick daily connection?
WhatWhich parts of the temple experience are we digitising: live streaming, interactive rituals, donations, or home delivery of prasad?
WhereWhere does it live: a mobile app for daily use, a website, or an immersive AR/VR experience?
WhenWhen is peak usage, and what's the launch timeline: big festivals like Diwali, or steady daily morning traffic?
WhyWhy is our company building this now: to monetise spiritual services, raise engagement, or remove geographic barriers for devotees?
HowHow do we bring in the supply side: partner with well-known temples, or create an independent virtual temple from scratch?

Product improvement: increase rides per user for Uber

An existing product and a clear metric, so the questions are about which users, which rides and why frequency is falling.

QuestionClarifying questions
WhoWhich segment do we focus on: daily office commuters, weekend socialisers, students, or airport-transfer users?
WhatWhat counts as a ride and what's the baseline? All categories (UberGo, Premium, Auto, Moto), or one tier such as UberPool?
WhereIs this a global goal or a specific geography, such as busy metro cities versus low-adoption Tier 2 cities?
WhenWhen is frequency dropping? Are people riding in the morning but switching to competitors or public transport in the evening, or leaving during surge hours?
WhyWhy now: losing share to Ola or Rapido, growing revenue, or improving driver utilisation by creating demand?
HowHow big an increase, and by when? For example, from 2 to 3 rides a week over the next quarter.
